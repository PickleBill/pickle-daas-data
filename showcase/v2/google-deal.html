Case study · the partnership

We didn't sell to Google.We built something it hadn't done.

As a sub-one-year-old startup, Dreamship closed a first-of-its-kind, cross-division Google partnership — not an acquisition, not a vendor contract. A BD deal that built Dreamship's brand, protected Google's, and gave underserved merchants access. Then Stripe, PayPal, Payoneer and Meta wanted in.

Role · Cofounder & CEO, in the room from first contactCompany · Dreamship

01 The opening

First contact came through an executive connection — a director — and I was in the room from the start. Dreamship had built a trademark & IP checker on Google Vision. Google wanted access to a large, untapped merchant market in a way that protected the Google brand. We brought the other half: high-volume merchants, verified and validated, driving real revenue for Google through Dreamship's trust layer.

The job wasn't a pitch. It was organizing the incentives so everyone in the room won — Google, the merchants, and us.

02 The room

The deal never almost died. As a startup nobody had heard of, I navigated 5–6 internal Google teams — trust & safety, business integrity, commercial — to director-plus sign-off. What I carried into each room was the same: a message that was passionate, persuasive, and committed, and a structure that made it win-win-win. Proof of commitment and aligned business incentives did the rest.

03 The flywheel

The Google partnership became credibility you could spend. Stripe, PayPal, Payoneer, Meta — and others — wanted the same access on the same terms. That's the thesis I keep coming back to: there's real edge in building business-development deals that are multi-party, complex, and have the right incentives aligned. Most people can sell one party. Far fewer can make three parties win at once.

04 The 11x year

It showed up in the numbers. Our first full year of business — a little COVID tailwind, mostly capitalizing on the opening in front of us: take in the orders, figure out the supply routes, meet the demand, and turn it into a trusted source for reliable shipping. Not one decision — a set of decisions, made fast, by a highly capable team. $35M+ revenue, 11x in a single year.

05 From the chemo chair

The clearest proof of the muscle came at the worst possible time. Mid-treatment — aggressive chemo at Duke hospital — I used my connections to get into Microsoft just as it began working with ChatGPT, and built a rev-share / go-to-market partnership: Dreamship merchants got ChatGPT access via Bing, Microsoft put real money behind the push, and we graduated into a higher partner tier on volume. Negotiated literally from a hospital chair, during treatment.

"I cut the Microsoft deal from a chair at Duke, mid-chemo." The pipeline didn't care that I was sick. Neither did I.
What this proves

I close complex, multi-party partnerships — and I'm still in the room.

Enterprise GTM that survives trust & safety, legal, and commercial review. Incentive design that makes everyone win. And the will to do it through anything. That's the work I want to bring inside a frontier lab.
